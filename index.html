Spotlight — Award

Dr Alastair Duncan — Outstanding Achiever in Dietetics Award 2026

We are delighted to congratulate Dr Alastair Duncan, Consultant Dietitian at Guy's and St Thomas' NHS Foundation Trust and Senior Lecturer in Nutrition and Dietetics at King's College London, on winning the Outstanding Achiever in Dietetics Award at the annual British Dietetic Association (BDA) Awards and Honours ceremony, held in Birmingham in June 2026.

The Outstanding Achiever Award recognises a person who has had a significant impact on the dietetics profession throughout their career and has been working in the profession for ten years or more. Alastair is the UK's first Consultant Dietitian in HIV care, having spent over 30 years advancing HIV nutrition, research and patient care.

His pioneering work in HIV nutrition, diabetes prevention and weight management has improved the lives of thousands of patients, while his research has advanced our understanding of metabolic health in people living with HIV. He has made an outstanding contribution to clinical practice, research and education across his career — a truly deserving recipient of this recognition.

Finalists: Beverley Beynon-Cobb, Sarah Fuller, Denise Kennedy.

Spotlight — Award

KHP DEO Team — Faculty of Life Sciences & Medicine Collaborative Research Excellence Award 2026

We are proud to share that the King's Health Partners Diabetes, Endocrinology and Obesity Team has been awarded the Collaborative Research Excellence Award at the Faculty of Life Sciences and Medicine Staff Awards 2026, in recognition of the team's contribution to the Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology Commission on Clinical Obesity.

The Commission, led by Professor Francesco Rubino of King's College London, published its landmark paper Definition and diagnostic criteria of clinical obesity in January 2025, reframing obesity as a chronic systemic disease with clear diagnostic criteria. Professor Rubino described this award as an achievement that "belongs to the entire DEO team and to everyone who contributed to the Commission over its long journey." The Commission report, Shaping a Healthier Future: The Power of Policy in Addressing the Obesity Crisis, was recently launched and has been covered by Politics UK.

Dr Kirsty Winkley — Diabetes Educator of the Year 2026

We are delighted to celebrate Dr Kirsty Winkley, Reader in Diabetes & Primary Care at King's College London, who has been named Diabetes Educator of the Year at the Diabetes Nursing Awards 2026.

Kirsty was recognised for her significant impact on improving diabetes education through her work as a clinician, researcher and educator, helping both healthcare professionals and people living with diabetes to build the knowledge and confidence needed to improve outcomes.

Speaking after receiving the award, Kirsty said: "It is a real honour, and I really appreciate being a diabetes educator. I am just absolutely delighted that what we do as a team can improve outcomes for people living with diabetes — and that is ultimately the aim of what we do."

The awards were held at The Orchard Hotel and Restaurant in Nottingham on 10 July 2026, hosted by broadcaster and GP Dr Sarah Jarvis MBE, and brought together around 100 healthcare professionals from across the UK to celebrate excellence in diabetes care.

Professor Michael Edmonds — King's College Hospital Quarterly Award

Professor Michael Edmonds has been presented with the King's College Hospital Quarterly Award in recognition of the 45th anniversary of the Diabetic Foot Clinic — a well-deserved honour celebrating decades of dedication, leadership and outstanding contributions to diabetic foot care.

New Models of Care

New Publication: Identifying Barriers to Diabetes Technology Use — LIFESTEPS

A new paper in Acta Diabetologica led by Geraldine Gallen, Type 1 Service Lead and Nurse Consultant in Diabetes at King's College Hospital, introduces LIFESTEPS — a newly developed, multilingual clinical support tool designed to help healthcare professionals explore psychosocial, behavioural, relational, and lifestyle-related barriers to diabetes technology use in people with type 1 diabetes.

The LIFESTEPS questionnaire was developed by a multidisciplinary expert panel from 11 countries across Europe, the Middle East and Africa, and covers three broad thematic areas: barriers to technology use, body image concerns, and therapy engagement and self-management. It is intended as a conversation-support tool for use in routine clinical practice — not a diagnostic instrument — to support more structured and person-centred discussions about diabetes technology.

A two-round end-user evaluation involving 19 healthcare professionals and 37 people with type 1 diabetes from across 10 countries found the questionnaire to be generally understandable, clinically relevant and potentially useful in practice. The paper notes that further psychometric and prospective evaluation will be required before broader clinical applications can be considered.

Hybrid closed-loop systems in people with type 1 diabetes and comorbid mental health conditions

A paper published in Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism (January 2026) by the King's College Hospital NHS FT diabetes team The study examined clinical outcomes of adults with type 1 diabetes and comorbid depression and/or anxiety disorders using hybrid closed-loop technology.

The findings highlight the benefits of a service model incorporating mental health professionals within the multidisciplinary diabetes team, and demonstrate the value of a robust audit culture and contributions of medical students engaged in clinical research activities.

KHP Diabetes Technology Foundation Educational Workshop — 6 July 2026

The King's Health Partners Diabetes Technology Foundation Educational Workshop took place on Monday 6 July 2026 at Bush House North, Strand Campus, London. The free full-day event brought together healthcare professionals from across the partnership for a packed programme of talks, hands-on rotations and case-based learning focused on diabetes technology in clinical practice.

The day opened with a welcome and introduction from Ben Yusuf, followed by sessions on how patients access diabetes technology (Geraldine Gallen and Siobhán Pender) and the relationship between diabetes technology and mental health (Katie Fitzgerald). A highlight of the morning was an extended interactive and immersive rotation programme, giving attendees hands-on experience with devices from myLife DiabetesCare, Dexcom, Insulet and Medtronic.

The afternoon featured case studies (Geraldine Gallen, Siobhán Pender, Liz Camfield, Alice Sones), patient perspectives on living with diabetes technology, and sessions on troubleshooting inpatient diabetes technology (Georgia Noble-Bell) and an overview of GSTT technology guidelines for hospital wards (Alice Sones).

Research Highlights

'Yo-yo' fluctuations in weight and blood pressure linked to kidney disease in type 1 diabetes

New research from Dr Murat Ozdede and Professor Janaka Karalliedde at King's College London has found that fluctuations — known as the 'yo-yo' effect — in body mass index (BMI) and blood pressure are associated with the progression of diabetic kidney disease in people with type 1 diabetes.

The findings, published in two studies, suggest that keeping fluctuations under control may be a more important protective factor than simply maintaining a good average blood pressure or weight. Diabetic Kidney Disease affects up to 40% of people with diabetes and is one of the leading causes of kidney failure in the UK.

More than half of women with gestational diabetes subjected to 'dangerous' stigma

New research led by King's College London and University College Cork, funded by Diabetes UK, has revealed the scale of stigma experienced by women with gestational diabetes (GDM). Researchers surveyed 1,800 women across the UK and conducted focus groups to understand the drivers of stigma and develop tools to address its harmful effects.

The survey found that over half of women felt judged because of their GDM, and more than 80% felt that others did not understand the condition. Stigma was also frequently experienced in healthcare settings. The researchers are calling for targeted interventions to reduce stigma, alongside structured emotional support for women during and after pregnancies affected by GDM.
